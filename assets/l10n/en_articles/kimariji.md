# The kimariji

The reader reads each poem from its first sound. You don't have to wait for the rest. As soon as you know which card it is, you can take it.

The first sounds that tell you are the card's kimariji. They are enough because no other card starts that way.

Card 1 starts with {あきの} and card 79 with {あきか}. Both start with {あき}, so {あき} is not enough. The next sound tells them apart. That's why the kimariji of card 1 is {あきの}.

![The red sound decides.](assets/artworks/articles/kimariji_pair.webp)

# Shorter as the match goes on

Each card is read only once. After that it's out of play. So once card 79 ({あきか}) has been read, card 1 is the only card left that starts with {あき}. Now {あき} is enough.

![One card is gone, so fewer sounds are enough.](assets/artworks/articles/kimariji_read.webp)

It goes on like this. 16 cards start with {あ}. Once the other 15 have all been read, the single sound {あ} is enough.

The stairs on a card's About page show this step by step. The top step is what you need at the start of a match. Each step down is a shorter sound. It is enough once the other cards on that step have been read.

>{.tobi pose=pointing} So remember which cards have been read. The more are gone, the less you need to hear!

# How to read the stairs

Here are the stairs of card 1.

![](assets/artworks/articles/kimariji_stairs.webp)

The Kimariji line above the stairs shows the card's whole kimariji. The stairs start from there.

## The sound

The big sound is how much you need to hear on that step.

## The badge

The badge counts the cards that start with that sound, this card included. Yellow means the sound is enough right now, because only this card starts that way. That's always the top step. Black marks a consonant step, which the next chapter explains. The other steps are white.

## The line

The line under the sound says how many cards start that way and how many of the others have to be read first.

## The chips

The chips are the cards that join on that step. They and the cards on the steps above must all be read before the sound is enough. A chip shows a card's kimariji and number. Tap one to open that card. If there are many, tap the last chip to see the rest.

Look at the step {あき}. Its badge says 2, because 2 cards start with {あき}. Its chip is card 79, {あきか}. Once card 79 has been read, {あき} is enough for card 1.

# Consonant steps

A sound like ka (か) is really two sounds, a k and then an a. The k reaches you first. If no other card left has that consonant there, you can go before the vowel.

A consonant step ends in a dash, with the consonant in romaji. {あk-} means {あ} followed by the consonant k.

That's why card 1's top step is {あきn-}. Card 79 has k there, not n.

Card 15's top step is {きみがためh-}. The only other card that starts with {きみがため} is card 50 ({きみがためお}), and it goes on with a vowel. So as soon as you hear the h, it can only be card 15.

![](assets/artworks/articles/kimariji_stairs_consonant.webp)

Below it, {きm-} works the same way. Once card 50 has been read, {きm-} is enough.

Sounds are counted as you hear them, not as they are written. The sounds sh, ch, ts, f and j each count as one. So card 3's top step is {あsh-}. Cards like {あさじ} have s there, and s is not sh.

>{.tobi pose=fired} Going on the consonant takes practice. Start with the cards you know best!

# Sounds or Kana

The switch above the stairs picks how they are drawn. Sounds, the one you start with, shows every step, consonant steps too.

Kana shows whole kana only, if you prefer to think in kana. The stairs start from the kimariji, and each step down is shorter by whole kana. The part you no longer need is drawn faded.

Here is card 1 again, with Kana.
