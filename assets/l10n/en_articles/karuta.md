# A hundred poems

Hyakunin Isshu means “one poem each from a hundred poets”. About 800 years ago the poet Fujiwara no Teika chose 100 short poems, each by a different person.

Every poem has 31 sounds in five parts of 5, 7, 5, 7 and 7. The first three parts are the first half. The last two are the second half.

![First half in black, second half in pink.](assets/artworks/articles/karuta_poem_halves.webp)

Many families play with these cards at New Year. Competitive karuta is the sport version. Two players kneel face to face and race to take the right card first.

# The reader and two kinds of cards

Every match has a reader. The reader doesn't play. They read the poems out loud, one at a time, in a random order.

There are two sets of 100 cards. A reading card (yomifuda) has the whole poem, and only the reader uses it. A taking card (torifuda) shows just the second half, written in hiragana. These are the cards on the floor.

![The reading card, which the reader reads, and the taking card, which you take.](assets/artworks/articles/karuta_two_cards.webp)

>{.tobi pose=pointing} So you hear how a poem starts and grab the card with how it ends!

# How a match goes

1. The 100 taking cards are mixed face down. Each player gets 25. The other 50 are left out.
2. You lay your 25 cards in three rows in front of you. That's your side. The other player's side is right across from it.
3. Then you have 15 minutes to remember where every card is, on both sides.
4. The reader starts with a warm-up poem that isn't one of the 100. Then the real cards begin.
5. When a poem is read, touch its card before the other player does. It can be on either side. Sweeping it off the field with your hand counts too.
6. If you take a card from their side, you send one of your own cards over to them.
7. The first player to clear their own side wins.

![Their side at the top, yours at the bottom. The pink card was just read.](assets/artworks/articles/karuta_field.webp)

>{.tobi pose=relaxed} Fifteen minutes only to remember? Yep. Good players know where all 50 cards are.

# Faults

Remember the 50 cards that were left out? The reader reads those too. They're called dead cards. If a dead card is read and you touch a card on the field, that's a fault.

Touching the wrong side is a fault as well. If the card read is on your side and you touch one on their side, it's a fault, and the same the other way round. Touching another card on the right side is fine.

After a fault, the other player sends you one of their cards. So you need to be quick, but also to wait for the right moment.

# Kimariji

Here's the big secret. You don't have to hear the whole poem. Its first few sounds tell you which card it is. Those sounds are called its kimariji, the deciding sounds.

## One-sound cards

Seven poems start with a sound no other poem starts with. Hear that one sound and you can go. Players remember them as {むすめふさほせ}.

![](assets/artworks/articles/karuta_one_sound.webp)

## When poems start the same

Most poems share their first sounds with another poem. {はるすぎて} and {はるのよの} both start with {はる}, so {はる} isn't enough. You have to wait for the third sound.

![The red sound decides. Both are 3-sound cards.](assets/artworks/articles/karuta_kimariji_long.webp)

## Kimariji change as you play

Each card is read only once. After {はるすぎて} has been read, {はるのよの} is the only card left that starts with {はる}. Now {はる} is enough, so it has become a 2-sound card.

![One is gone, so two sounds are enough now.](assets/artworks/articles/karuta_kimariji_change.webp)

The longest kimariji have six sounds. {あさぼらけ}, {わたのはら} and {きみがため} each start two poems, and only the sixth sound tells them apart.

>{.tobi pose=fired} Kimariji are the heart of karuta. Know them well and your hand moves before the poem ends!

# How Fudatobashi helps

This app trains the part that matters most. A card appears, you say its kimariji and swipe it away as fast as you can. Every swipe is timed.

On the journey, new cards come in a few at a time, and cards you're about to forget come back at the right moment. Once you know all 100, take the graduation exam. Pass it and ranked mode opens, where your speed sets your rank.
