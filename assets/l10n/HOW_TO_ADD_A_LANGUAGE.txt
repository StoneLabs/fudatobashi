To add a UI language:

1. Copy en.json to <code>.json (e.g. fr.json for French), next to it.
2. Translate every value. Keep the {0}, {1}, {band} … markers exactly where
   they are — they get filled in with numbers or names at runtime.
3. Set "languageName" to the language's own name, e.g. "Français".
4. Add "<code>" to languages.json.
5. Rebuild the app.

A key ending in "One" (e.g. cardsCountOne next to cardsCount) is the text
for a count of exactly 1, the singular in English ("1 card"). A language
without a singular form repeats the other text there.

kimariji001–kimariji100 are a special case: one per poem (by its id, zero-
padded to 3 digits), for each card's kimariji (決まり字). These are
READINGS, not spellings — how the card actually sounds in competitive
karuta, not a letter-by-letter transliteration of its hiragana. en.json's
are lowercase Hepburn romaji (macrons for long vowels, e.g. "ōe"); ja.json's
are the hiragana a Japanese reader would say aloud, which can differ from
the poem's own historical-kana spelling (`kimariji` in poems.json) — see
each poem's `kimarijiReading` in assets/data/poems.json, which is what both
of these are built from (and what scripts/kimariji_voice.py synthesizes
audio from). A new language should translate the *reading*, e.g. the
pronunciation in its own script or romanization, not copy en.json's romaji
as if it were English text.

Long texts are Markdown files in one folder per language, <code>_articles/:
  rules.md                 the competition rules
  teiichi.md               the 定位置 guide
  card_details/000.md …    each card's explanation on its "About this card"
  card_details/100.md      page (000 is the 序歌)
For a new language, copy en_articles/ to <code>_articles/ and translate the
files; add the folder and its card_details/ folder to the assets list in
pubspec.yaml. A file that is missing shows the English one.

Besides plain Markdown, these files may hold:

  >{.tobi pose=waving} Text
      Tobi says the text in a speech balloon. The pose is optional
      (standard when left out) and is the name of one of Tobi's pictures in
      assets/graphics/tobi/: standard, waving, cheering, pointing, relaxed,
      shocked, try-hard, fired. An unknown pose shows the standard one. A
      quote without {.tobi} stays a normal quote.

  ![Caption under the picture](assets/artworks/articles/name.webp)
      A picture, alone on its line, as wide as the text, with the caption
      under it. Pictures are shared by all languages, so translate only the
      caption; keep words out of the pictures themselves where you can. A
      picture may have versions, which the app picks by itself:
        name.ja.webp          for one language (English stands in for a
                              language without its own)
        name_hiragana.webp    for players who read kana
        name_romaji.webp      for players who read romaji
        name_romaji.ja.webp   for both at once
      Always write the plain name (name.webp) in the text.

  {あきの}
      Kana in curly braces, written as they sound ({わ} for the particle
      は). Players who read kana see あきの; players who read romaji see
      akino, or, in the karuta guide, あきの (akino). Braces that start with
      a dot, like {.tobi}, are something else. Write \{ for a real brace.

  <!-- a note -->
      A note on lines of its own, for writers only: the app leaves it out.

Japanese text may mark where its phrases end with a zero-width space
(U+200B), like some lines in ja.json; the app then breaks its lines only
there. Mark all of a file or none of it.
