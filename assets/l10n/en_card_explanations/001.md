# Explanation

## What the poem says

A rough hut stands by a field of ripe rice, put up for the harvest. Its roof is rush matting with a loose weave, so the night dew drips through. *My sleeves are wet with dew, again and again.*

```verse
[秋|あき]の[田|た]の　かりほの[庵|いほ]の
[苫|とま]をあらみ
わが[衣手|ころもで]は　[露|つゆ]にぬれつつ
```

### Words to know

- **かりほ** (kariho): a hut put up for a short time. It can also be heard as 刈り穂, the cut ears of rice.
- **苫** (toma): matting woven from rushes, used as a roof.
- **をあらみ** (o arami): "because it is coarse". The form を + adjective stem + み gives a reason.
- **衣手** (koromode): sleeves.
- **つつ** (tsutsu): the action goes on, again and again.

## The poet

Emperor Tenji (天智天皇, 626–672) was the 38th emperor of Japan. As Prince Naka no Ōe, he led the Taika Reform with Nakatomi no Kamatari from 645. He moved the capital to Ōmi, by Lake Biwa, and took the throne there in 668.

> The poem may not be his own. The Man'yōshū has a very similar poem by an unknown author. The Gosen Wakashū gives this version under the emperor's name.

### Why it comes first

Fujiwara no Teika put the 100 poems roughly in order of time, so the oldest poets come first. Many readers also see an emperor who feels for the farmers' hard work, which makes it a fitting start.

## In a match

1. The kimariji is **あきの**: three sounds. Only one other card starts with あき: **あきかぜに** (#79).
2. The taking card starts **わがころもで**, like the one for #15 **きみがためは**. Learn the two together.
3. Ōmi Jingū in Ōtsu honours Emperor Tenji. The national karuta championships for the Meijin and Queen titles are held there every January.
