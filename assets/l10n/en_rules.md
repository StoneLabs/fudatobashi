# Chapter 1 | General provisions

## Article 1 | Method of play

**1** A match is played with Ogura Hyakunin Isshu karuta, between two players seated facing each other.

**2** Each player takes as their own cards (mochifuda) 25 cards chosen at random from the 100 taking cards (torifuda). The players compete to take the cards the reader reads out (hereinafter “the card read”, defuda), and the player whose own cards run out first is the winner.

> **Supplementary notes**
>
> ○ The basic idea is to reduce one's own cards by taking cards, but one's own cards may also increase because of a fault (otetsuki).
>
> ○ The winner must always bring their own cards down to 0 before ending the match. In particular, if no card is sent over (okurifuda) in a case such as 1–1, both players may end the match each believing they took the card, and problems may arise afterwards.

## Article 2 | Rulings

Rulings on takes, faults and the like are, as a rule, decided between the players.

> **Supplementary notes**
>
> ○ In competitive karuta, the principle is that players watch each other's movements closely, state their claims calmly in the spirit of good faith and sincerity, and settle problems quickly.
>
> ○ Where needed, for example on the interpretation of these Regulations, a player may confirm the matter with a referee.
>
> ○ If the two players cannot reach a decision by discussion, either player may ask a referee for a ruling, but the ruling the referee gives must be followed.
>
> ○ Even if a referee makes a clearly wrong ruling, that ruling shall be followed. Doubts about the interpretation or application of the Regulations may be referred to the Competitive Karuta Department afterwards, but even then, past rulings will not be overturned.

# Chapter 2 | Etiquette

## Article 3 | Etiquette

During competition, players must respect each other and value etiquette highly.

## Article 4 | Bowing

**1** At the start and at the end of a match, players shall bow, first to the opponent and then to the reader.

> **Supplementary notes**
>
> ○ When a referee is assigned to an individual match, players shall bow to the opponent, the referee and the reader, in that order.
>
> ○ When bowing, players turn their whole body towards the other person, look at their face, say clearly “Onegai shimasu” (お願いします) and “Arigato gozaimashita” (ありがとうございました), and lower their head.
>
> ○ If a referee joins partway through a match, players shall bow to the referee at that point.
>
> ○ Referees shall also bow together with the players.

**2** The reader shall also bow once to the hall at the start and at the end of a match.

**3** When leaving their place or returning to it during memorisation time or during a match, players bow to the opponent. This does not apply, however, when going to fetch a card that has been swept away.

> **Supplementary notes**
>
> ○ Players bow saying “Shitsurei shimasu” (失礼します) and “Shitsurei shimashita” (失礼しました).
>
> ○ The person receiving the bow need only lower their head lightly.

## Article 5 | Sitting posture

While seated in the competition area, players must sit in seiza (formal kneeling) or a posture close to seiza. During memorisation time, however, they may relax their posture.

> **Supplementary notes**
>
> ○ Even when relaxing their posture during memorisation time, players must not sit with a knee raised, sit with their knees drawn up to the chest, stretch their legs out, or the like.

## Article 6 | Clothing and things worn

**1** For competition, players must wear clothing that does not cause discomfort to the opponent or to spectators.

> **Supplementary notes**
>
> ○ Japanese dress is preferred, but where a tournament or the like gives no particular instructions, T-shirts, sweatshirts, sportswear and the like are acceptable. However, shorts, clothes cut low at the chest and the like are not desirable.

**2** Nothing that could injure the opponent during a match, such as artificial nails or rings, may be worn on the valid hand (yukote).

> **Supplementary notes**
>
> ○ The nails of the valid hand shall be trimmed short and even as far as possible. The valid hand shall be bare, but taping and adhesive bandages are allowed.

**3** Anything that swings in front of the opponent shall be removed or fixed in place.

> **Supplementary notes**
>
> ○ This covers not only necklaces and earrings but also hairstyles: if hair swings in front of the opponent, it shall be fixed in place, for example by tying it back.

# Chapter 3 | Card layout and memorisation

## Article 7 | Boundary lines

**1** In front of where they sit, each player marks out an area 87 centimetres wide and as deep as three cards placed one above another, with 1 centimetre between each of the top, middle and bottom rows; this is each player's side (jin). Each edge of the outer border of each side is called a boundary line (kyogisen).

> **Supplementary notes**
>
> ○ Boundary lines do not mean only the part touching the tatami; they also include the space vertically above it.
>
> ○ The area enclosed by the boundary lines is thus in two parts: one's own side (hereinafter “own side”, jijin) and the opponent's side (hereinafter “opponent's side”, aitejin).
>
> ○ The figures of 87 centimetres, 3 centimetres and 1 centimetre in this Article do not mean that being off by even 1 millimetre is a violation, but players should check in everyday practice roughly how long these lengths are.

**2** The gap between the top rows of the two sides shall be 3 centimetres, and the extensions of the left and right boundary lines shall line up.

> **Supplementary notes**
>
> ○ When the cards are laid out along the weave of the tatami, the gap between the two sides' top-row boundary lines becomes somewhat wider than 3 centimetres, but this is interpreted as leaving three strands of the weave between them. This does not apply, however, where one strand of the weave is clearly 1.5 centimetres or more.

## Article 8 | Own cards

**1** The players turn the cards given to the field face down and mix them; then, still face down, each chooses 25 cards as their own cards.

**2** When either player's own cards, or both players', are not 25, the surplus or shortfall may be adjusted, but only until the end of memorisation time.

> **Supplementary notes**
>
> ○ The players must confirm with each other during memorisation time that each has 25 own cards.
>
> ○ A surplus or shortfall is adjusted by reporting it to a referee, under the referee's instructions.
>
> ○ If a surplus or shortfall of own cards comes to light after memorisation time has ended, the number of cards is not adjusted, and the match continues with the cards as they are.

## Article 9 | Laying out own cards

Players lay out all their own cards face up, with the writing facing themselves, without overlapping, neatly, in any position within their own side. However, cards must not be laid out straddling the top, middle and bottom rows.

## Article 10 | Moving own cards

**1** When moving their own cards, players must tell the opponent each time.

> **Supplementary notes**
>
> ○ When moving cards, the player must announce it clearly, and the opponent receiving the announcement must also reply clearly.
>
> ○ Failing to announce a move of own cards is not at once treated as a violation or as invalid. However, where failures to announce are repeated, or are deliberate, it is left to the referee's judgement.
>
> ○ Frequent moves, or moving a large number of cards at once, are not desirable, memorisation time included.

**2** When a player closes up neighbouring cards sideways after taking the card read or moving a card, this notice may be omitted.

**3** Once the reading of the second half (shimo no ku) has begun, players may not move cards.

## Article 11 | Memorisation time

**1** After the players have laid out their own cards, and before the match begins, 15 minutes of memorisation time is taken.

**2** Until 2 minutes of memorisation time remain, players must not do anything that hinders the opponent's memorisation, such as memorising by moving their hands or practice swings (suburi).

> **Supplementary notes**
>
> ○ Acts that hinder memorisation include not only practice swings and hitting the tatami, but also memorising while frequently reaching out or swinging one's hands.
>
> ○ A player who does practice swings, warm-ups or the like before only 2 minutes of memorisation time remain shall bow to the opponent, leave their seat, and do them somewhere else.

## Article 12 | Tidying the cards

When cards that were laid out are scattered, for example by a take, as a rule the player who swept the cards must go and pick them up.

> **Supplementary notes**
>
> ○ The player who swept the cards goes to pick them up, but the opponent also helps as far as possible. While doing so, players must strictly refrain from walking inside other players' boundary lines.
>
> ○ When handing scattered cards to another player, hand them over politely.
>
> ○ Tidying the cards is done seated; players shall refrain from postures such as sitting cross-legged, sitting with a knee raised, or half-standing.
>
> ○ Avoid disputes while the cards are not laid out; tidy the cards first, and then discuss.

# Chapter 4 | Stance

## Article 13 | Stance

**1** Each player chooses either the left or the right hand as the hand that takes cards (hereinafter “valid hand”, yukote). Until the reading of the first half (kami no ku) begins, the valid hand must be kept touching the tatami, nearer to the player than the bottom row of their own side, and the head must not come out past the top row of their own side towards the opponent.

> **Supplementary notes**
>
> ○ A referee may caution a player for any act contrary to this Article, even without an appeal from the opponent.
>
> ○ “Nearer than the bottom row of their own side” includes the space above it. Also, not only the valid hand but also the other hand and both legs must be nearer.
>
> ○ “Head” here includes the hair.

**2** The valid hand is all the fingers, the palm and the back of either the left or the right hand, from the wrist on; players may not switch the valid hand between left and right during a match.

> **Supplementary notes**
>
> ○ After the match begins, the hand that first takes a card, or the hand that first commits a fault, becomes the valid hand. However, if at that first take or fault the player tells the opponent that their valid hand is the other hand, and that this take or fault was made with the hand opposite the valid hand, it is handled accordingly.

**3** Until the reading of the first half begins, players must not put their valid hand outside the extensions of the left and right boundary lines.

**4** Once the reading has entered the lingering end (yoin) of the second half, players must not make large movements that would disturb the opponent.

> **Supplementary notes**
>
> ○ Players shall be in the stance set out in this Article before the lingering end of the second half.

# Chapter 5 | Reading

## Article 14 | Reading

**1** The reader reads out, one at a time, cards chosen at random from the 100 reading cards (yomifuda), and never reads the same card twice.

**2** The next card to be read is decided by the reader each time, just before reading it.

> **Supplementary notes**
>
> ○ The reader must take great care, for example by keeping unread cards in a box, that nobody can know which card will be read next.

**3** When reading out a card, the reader reads the second half of the card read before it, and then reads the first half of the next card.

> **Supplementary notes**
>
> ○ Starting to read the next card the moment a reading ends shall be avoided.
>
> ○ The reader must not start reading until it can be judged that the players have finished tidying the cards and sending cards and are ready for the next card.

**4** Before reading the first card, the reader reads out, as the opening poem (joka), a tanka that is not in the Ogura Hyakunin Isshu: after reading its first half and second half in succession, the reader reads the second half once more, and then reads the first half of the first card.

**5** As a rule, the reader shall not interrupt the reading because of minor noises or the like. However, where it is judged that there is a serious obstacle to the match, the reading may be interrupted.

> **Supplementary notes**
>
> ○ Players shall assume that the reading will not be interrupted, even if there are noises or the like.

**6** Except where unavoidable, the reader may not be replaced or change position partway through.

## Article 15 | When a reading counts

**1** The reading counts once the reader has read up to the kimariji, and takes and faults on that card then count.

> **Supplementary notes**
>
> ○ Kimariji here means the sounds that identify the one card read. For example, even if “あはじ” (awaji) and “あはれ” (aware) are on the same side, if only “あは” (awa) has been read, the one card read cannot be identified, so the reading does not count. No exception is made even when there is only one pair playing.
>
> ○ If the reading stops partway, or the voice is markedly poor, the chief referee may declare that the reading does not count.

**2** Even if a card whose reading has already counted is read again by mistake, if the reading has reached the kimariji of a card not yet read, the reading of that card is held to count. Any faults at that time are all invalid.

> **Supplementary notes**
>
> ○ For example, if “あはじ” (awaji) is on the field and “あはれ” (aware) has already been read, even if “あはれ” is read again, the reading is valid from the point where “あは” (awa) has been read, and “あはじ” is held to have been read.

## Article 16 | Stopping the reading

**1** As a rule, players may not make the reading wait except for tidying the cards.

> **Supplementary notes**
>
> ○ It is desirable to send cards before tidying the cards, also so as to prevent the reading from starting while cards are being tidied.
>
> ○ The cases other than tidying the cards in which the reader needs to be asked to wait are limited to cases where interrupting the match is objectively and reasonably necessary (for example, to deal with an injury).

**2** When making the reading wait, players must signal clearly to the reader by raising a hand or in words. However, they must not make it wait longer than necessary.

> **Supplementary notes**
>
> ○ When one player is tidying the cards, it is desirable that the opponent raise a hand to signal the reader. However, a player who has not finished sending a card shall raise their own hand.

**3** Once the reading of the second half has begun, players must not stop the reading. This does not apply, however, where a player had a hand raised but the reader started reading without noticing it.

> **Supplementary notes**
>
> ○ Even if a player notices a misplaced card, a lost card, a card not sent or the like after the reading of the second half has begun, they must not stop the reading.

# Chapter 6 | Taking

## Article 17 | When a take counts

**1** While the card read is inside the boundary lines, the player who touches it directly with the valid hand before the opponent takes the card read. (Direct take)

> **Supplementary notes**
>
> ○ Where cards lie fully on top of each other with the card read underneath, and the card read is still inside the boundary lines, touching the card on top does not make a take unless the card read itself is touched directly.

**2** Even when neither player made a direct take, a player who pushes the card read completely outside the boundary lines with the valid hand takes that card. (Push-out take)

> **Supplementary notes**
>
> ○ A push-out take that crosses between one's own side and the opponent's side is also valid. Thus, where a player trying to take the card read in the opponent's top row mistakenly pushes up from a card in their own top row, and the card read goes outside the boundary lines without the opponent touching it, the result is a fault on a card of one's own side together with a push-out take, the so-called “torizon” (a take that gains nothing).
>
> ○ If, during a push-out, the card read catches on the edge of the tatami, a player's knee or the like and does not go outside the boundary lines, the push-out take is likewise held not to count.

**3** If, after a push-out has been made, the opponent makes a direct take before the card read has gone completely outside the boundary lines, the direct take is valid.

**4** When both players push the card out in the same direction, the take goes to the player who touched it nearer to the card read.

**5** When push-out takes are in different directions, the take goes to the player who finally pushed the card read outside the boundary lines.

> **Supplementary notes**
>
> ○ Push-out takes in different directions are cases such as a sideways push-sweep against an upward push from below; whether the hand that began the sweep or the push was near to or far from the card read does not necessarily decide the final ruling on the take.

## Article 18 | Simultaneous takes

**1** When both players make a direct take and touch the card read at the same time, the player who had the card read among their own cards takes it.

**2** When both players make a push-out take and it cannot be judged whose take it is, the player who had the card read among their own cards takes it.

> **Supplementary notes**
>
> ○ This gives the take to the side the card was on, in cases so close that it cannot be judged whose take it is even in the light of paragraphs 4 and 5 of the preceding Article.

## Article 19 | Takes when a card is lost

**1** If one of a player's own cards that had stayed lost becomes the card read, the take goes to the opponent.

> **Supplementary notes**
>
> ○ When a lost card becomes the card read, all faults are invalid (no faults).
>

**2** If the two players' claims differ about which side a lost card read was on, or neither remembers clearly, a referee decides.

## Article 20 | Takes when a card is misplaced

**1** Even if a card laid out in the wrong place within the same side is read, the layout at the time of reading is valid, and takes, faults and so on are judged on that basis.

> **Supplementary notes**
>
> ○ Players must always check the layout of both sides' cards.

**2** If one of a player's own cards has been placed on the opponent's side by mistake and that card is read, the take is treated in the same way as a lost card, and any faults at that time are invalid.

## Article 21 | Invalid takes

If any of the following obstructive acts is committed, that take is made invalid each time, and the take goes to the opponent.

**(1)** Putting the valid hand inside the boundary lines before the reading of the first half begins.

**(2)** Obstructing the opponent's take, for example by gripping part of the opponent's body.

**(3)** Touching the card read directly with the hand opposite the valid hand (hereinafter “invalid hand”, mukote).

**(4)** Any other act recognised as obstructing the opponent.

> **Supplementary notes**
>
> ○ This applies when the player who committed the obstructive act took the card read, and does not apply when they did not take it; but the obstructive act itself may, depending on its degree and how often it happens, be subject to a caution, a warning or expulsion by a referee.
>
> ○ (3) Putting the card read outside the boundary lines with the invalid hand is also obstruction.
>
> ○ Whether a case where the opponent's take was hindered, for example by a kimono or a knee blocking the card read, is recognised as an obstructive act is judged by a referee on a player's appeal.
>
> ○ Whether or not the card read is on the field, if an obstructive act such as a false start (furaingu) occurs, faults by the player who committed the act are valid, and faults by the player who did not are invalid.
>
> *[Notice of June 2017 (Heisei 29), applied from July 2017 (Heisei 29)]*

## Article 22 | Special cases of takes

**1** If the card read goes outside the boundary lines through force majeure, the player who had the card read among their own cards takes it.

> **Supplementary notes**
>
> ○ Card movement by force majeure means cases such as a card sent flying by a neighbouring player hitting it.

**2** Even in a case of force majeure, if the card read stays within its original side, a take is made by touching the card read or by putting it out with a push-out.

# Chapter 7 | Faults

## Article 23 | Faults

**1** Touching with the valid hand a card on a side where the card read is not, while that card is inside the boundary lines (including the space above), is a fault (otetsuki).

> **Supplementary notes**
>
> ○ Touching a card other than the card read on the side where the card read is, is not a fault.
>
> ○ It is also a fault when a card moves towards a player, for example because the opponent swept it, and touches the valid hand while it has not yet fully left the boundary lines. However, it is not a fault if the valid hand it touched had stayed on the tatami since the stance.

**2** Even if the valid hand, while still touching a card on one side, carries that card into the other side, where it comes into contact with a card of the other side, it is not a fault on the other side unless the valid hand touches the card of the other side.

**3** Whether or not there was an intention to touch a card, touching a card in the course of one continuous motion of taking a card is a fault.

> **Supplementary notes**
>
> ○ Even when a player was only drawing back the hand that took a card on the opponent's side, touching a card of their own side in that continuous motion is a fault.
>
> ○ Touching a card by mistake at the end of a taking motion, while meaning to hit the tatami, is also regarded as part of the continuous motion, and is a fault.

**4** Touching a card clearly in the course of tidying the cards or the like is not regarded as a fault.

**5** Touching a card with anything other than the valid hand is not a fault.

**6** When a reading does not count, all faults are invalid.

## Article 24 | Double faults

**1** When a player is made to commit a fault through contact with the opponent, both players are held to have committed a fault (double fault, tomo-otetsuki).

> **Supplementary notes**
>
> ○ This means cases where a player is made to commit a fault because contact with the opponent physically changed the path of their hand.
>
> ○ Where the hand that faulted hits the opponent's hand after leaving the card, and the opponent did not touch a card, it is not a double fault.

**2** Even after contact with the opponent, a fault a player commits through their own movement is not a double fault.

> **Supplementary notes**
>
> ○ Even if contact with the opponent was the cause, a fault committed by moving the hand reflexively is not a double fault.

**3** If the opponent's hand touches the hand of a player who has committed a fault and is still touching the card, it is a double fault. This does not apply, however, where it is judged from the path of the hand that it clearly would not have touched the card.

# Chapter 8 | Cards sent over

## Article 25 | Sending a card

**1** When a player takes the card read on the opponent's side, or when the opponent commits a fault, the player may send one of their own cards over to the opponent.

> **Supplementary notes**
>
> ○ A card sent over shall be sent into the opponent's side, facing the opponent.
>
> ○ When sending a card, players must not do so sitting cross-legged, with a knee raised, half-standing or the like; they shall sit properly again and send it squarely towards the opponent.
>
> ○ For the progress of the match, there is no need to wait until the opponent has finished tidying the cards before sending a card.

**2** When a player takes the card read on the opponent's side and the opponent has also committed a fault, the player may send two cards.

> **Supplementary notes**
>
> ○ When sending two cards, send them one at a time, without overlapping them.

**3** When the card read is on neither side and the opponent commits faults on cards of both sides, the player may send two cards.

**4** Where both players may send cards, such as after a double fault, or when a player takes the card read on the opponent's side and commits a fault on a card of their own side, only the number left after offsetting the cards each would send may be sent.

**5** If a card is not sent before the reading of the second half begins, the right to send it is lost.

> **Supplementary notes**
>
> ○ This does not apply, even if the reader has begun reading the second half, where the player meant to send a card and had raised a hand to stop the reading, but the reader began reading without noticing.

**6** Even if a player sends a card by mistake without the right to send it, the card sent over becomes valid if the reading of the second half begins without either player noticing.

## Article 26 | Choosing the card to send

**1** The card to send is chosen freely by the sender. However, the card sent over cannot be changed from the moment the hand leaves it.

**2** The card to send shall be chosen promptly, without needlessly long thought.

**3** If, by mistake, two cards are sent where one should have been, the card sent first is valid.

> **Supplementary notes**
>
> ○ Only where the two cards were sent at the same time, either card may be made the card sent over.

# Chapter 9 | Other matters

## Article 27 | Prohibited acts

Players must not do any of the following.

**(1)** Making sounds or hitting the tatami once the reading has entered the lingering end of the second half.

**(2)** Eating, drinking or smoking during a match.

**(3)** Hitting the tatami more than necessary during a match.

**(4)** Complaining about the reader's habits of pronunciation, unclear sounds or the like.

**(5)** Checking with the opponent or anyone else about kimariji, the card read, reading cards or cards sent over.

**(6)** Improper words or behaviour towards the opponent, other players, the reader or referees, such as intimidation or trying to put them off.

**(7)** Cheering on players, by players or spectators.

**(8)** Not following a referee's rulings or instructions.

> **Supplementary notes**
>
> ○ (3) Even without hitting the tatami, players shall not make excessively frequent practice swings.
>
> ○ (5) As a rule, during a match, players must not talk with other players or spectators, about the match of course, and about anything else as well. If the reading stopped partway, or could not be heard because of noise or the like, players may ask a referee to confirm, as far as that card is concerned, but cannot confirm anything about other cards.
>
> ○ (7) Except where some cheering is allowed, such as in team matches, as a rule nothing that would be regarded as cheering may be done.
>
> ○ A player who commits a prohibited act is subject, at the referee's judgement, to a caution, a warning or expulsion.
>
> ○ A caution points out the violation and urges the player to correct it. A warning is given on the understanding that a further violation will lead to expulsion. Expulsion means stopping the match partway, ruling it lost by a foul, and ordering the player to leave the competition area.
>
> ○ Referees may take the same measures against spectators.

## Article 28 | Supplementary provisions

**1** Matters not provided for in these Regulations are left to the judgement of the chief referee.

**2** These Regulations may be updated by the All Japan Karuta Association, a general incorporated association, as needed.
